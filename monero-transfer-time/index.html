How Long Does Monero Take to Transfer

A Monero transfer takes about 20 minutes to become fully spendable, because the network requires 10 confirmations and each block lands roughly every 2 minutes.

The Short Answer: ~20 Minutes to Spendable

A Monero transfer is received almost immediately but takes about 20 minutes — ten confirmations — before the recipient can spend it. Monero targets one block every two minutes, so your transaction is normally mined into the next block within a minute or two of broadcasting. At that point the recipient's wallet shows the incoming payment, but it displays as locked. The protocol holds every newly received output for 10 blocks, and only after that tenth block, roughly 20 minutes later, does the balance become usable. This 10-confirmation rule is not a wallet setting or an exchange policy; it is enforced by the Monero network itself, which is why the number is so consistent regardless of the amount sent or the wallet used.

Why 10 Confirmations and Not Fewer

Most people expect one or two confirmations to be enough, the way many chains treat a payment as settled quickly. Monero is deliberately stricter, and the reason comes down to how its outputs are locked and how deep a reorganization the network wants to rule out before letting coins move again.

The 10-Block Lock on Every Output

Every output you receive carries a built-in 10-block lock time before it can be used as an input to a new transaction. This is a consensus rule, so no wallet can shorten it. The lock exists partly to protect against chain reorganizations: if a short fork were to reverse a block, coins that had already been respent could cause problems, and a 10-block depth makes a reversal that deep extremely unlikely. The rule is documented across the Monero project resources and is identical for every transaction on the network.

Depth Versus Speed

Ten blocks at a two-minute target works out to roughly twenty minutes of settlement depth. Compared with chains that settle in seconds, this feels slow, but it buys a strong guarantee that the funds you receive cannot be clawed back by a reorg before you respend them. Because the lock applies to the output rather than to the wallet, receiving several payments means each one unlocks on its own 10-block schedule from when it was mined.

Fee Priority and How Fast You Get Into a Block

The one part of the timing you can influence is how quickly your transaction is included in a block. Monero wallets expose a fee priority selector, and a higher priority pays more per byte so miners include your transaction sooner when the mempool is busy. The priority does not change the 10-confirmation unlock; it only changes how long you wait for that first confirmation. When the network is quiet, even the default priority lands in the next block, so paying extra buys nothing. During congestion, a higher priority is what keeps your first confirmation close to the two-minute target instead of slipping to a later block.

What Can Slow a Monero Transfer Down

Most transfers hit the ~20-minute mark reliably, but a few conditions stretch it. Knowing them helps you tell a normal wait from a genuine problem.

A Fee Set Too Low During Congestion

If you choose the lowest priority while the mempool is full, your transaction can sit unconfirmed for several blocks until miners clear higher-paying transactions ahead of it. The fix is to pick a higher priority before sending; once broadcast, a transaction's fee cannot be raised on Monero the way a replace-by-fee bump works elsewhere.

Block Time Variance

Two minutes is a target, not a guarantee. Blocks are found by a random process, so one block might arrive in 30 seconds and the next in four minutes. Over ten confirmations these swings average out, but any single transfer can finish a little faster or slower than the round 20-minute figure.

Wallet Sync and Node Choice

Sometimes the delay is on your side rather than the network's. A wallet that is still syncing, or a remote node that is lagging, can make a confirmed transfer look pending in your interface. Letting the wallet finish scanning the latest blocks, or switching to a well-synced node, usually resolves an apparent stall that the network already cleared.

Unlock Time Versus Confirmation Time

Two phrases get mixed up around Monero timing: confirmations and unlock time. A confirmation is simply a block mined on top of the one containing your transaction. The unlock time is the point at which an output can be spent, which for an ordinary transfer is exactly 10 confirmations.
  • Confirmation: each new block stacked above your transaction's block, arriving about every two minutes.
  • Default unlock: every received output unlocks after 10 confirmations, roughly 20 minutes.
  • Custom unlock: a sender can set a longer unlock time on an output, delaying spendability beyond the standard 10 blocks, though this is rare for everyday transfers.
  • Coinbase outputs: mined block rewards use a much longer 60-block lock, but that only affects miners, not normal payments.

Transfer Time When Swapping XMR to Another Coin

When you swap Monero for another asset rather than just sending it, the total time is the sum of two independent legs, and the Monero leg is usually the one that sets the pace. The XMR side needs its 10 confirmations, about 20 minutes, before a careful counterpart or swap tool will release the destination coin. The destination leg then adds its own chain's confirmation time on top.

The XMR Leg Sets the Floor

Because a swap provider will not hand over the other asset until your Monero is settled to the standard depth, plan on the full ~20-minute XMR confirmation window before the second leg even begins. Paying a higher fee priority on the Monero side shaves the wait to the first confirmation but does not change the 10-block requirement.

Adding the Destination Leg

If you are receiving Bitcoin, add Bitcoin's confirmation time, commonly one to six blocks at roughly ten minutes each, so an XMR-to-BTC swap can run from about half an hour to an hour end to end. A fast chain like Litecoin adds only a few minutes, while a slow or congested destination adds more. In every case the two legs run back to back, so the realistic total is the XMR 20 minutes plus whatever the receiving chain needs.

How long does Monero take to transfer?
About 20 minutes to become fully spendable. The payment reaches the recipient's wallet within the first block, usually under two minutes, but Monero requires 10 confirmations before the funds can be spent.
Why does Monero need 10 confirmations?
Every received output carries a 10-block lock enforced by consensus. The depth protects against chain reorganizations, making it extremely unlikely that a received payment could be reversed before you respend it.
Can I make a Monero transfer faster?
You can speed up how quickly your transaction gets into a block by choosing a higher fee priority, which matters when the mempool is busy. You cannot shorten the 10-confirmation unlock, which is fixed for every transfer.
What is Monero's block time?
Monero targets one block roughly every two minutes. Individual blocks vary because mining is random, but over ten confirmations the average holds close to 20 minutes.
Why does my wallet show the payment as locked?
A received output displays as locked until it reaches 10 confirmations. The wallet sees the incoming amount right away but keeps it unspendable until the 10-block lock clears, about 20 minutes after broadcast.
How long does an XMR to another coin swap take?
Count both legs. The Monero side needs its ~20-minute confirmation window before the destination coin is released, then the receiving chain adds its own confirmation time, so an XMR-to-BTC swap often runs from about 30 minutes to an hour.
What slows a Monero transfer down?
A fee set too low during congestion, natural variance in block times, or a wallet that is still syncing. The first is the most common and is avoided by choosing a higher priority before sending.
