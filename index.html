What is it

What a Monero swap is

A Monero swap exchanges XMR for another coin, or another coin for XMR. This interface supports XMR against BTC, ETH and USDT, and you receive the result at an address in your own wallet.

Why it works differently from a DEX swap

Monero runs on its own blockchain. It is not an EVM chain, so there are no smart-contract pools or token approvals to swap against. Moving value between XMR and another asset therefore needs either a service that holds liquidity on both sides, or a protocol that links two separate chains directly.

Two routes

An instant-exchange route has you send coins to a deposit address and receive the other coin at your own address. A BTC–XMR atomic swap pairs you with a counterparty and settles both sides without a trusted middle party. Learn more about the coin itself in the official Monero introduction.

Instant vs atomic

Instant exchange vs atomic swap

Both routes end with coins in your wallet, but they differ in who you rely on while the swap is in progress and in which pairs are possible.

Which to choose

If you need ETH or USDT, or want fewer steps, the instant-exchange route is the practical option. If you are swapping BTC and XMR and prefer not to depend on a service during the trade, an atomic swap removes that dependency in exchange for more time and a counterparty with enough liquidity.

How to swap

How to swap Monero

No wallet connection or signing here

Monero does not use browser-wallet signing the way EVM chains do. Instead you give the interface a receiving address and send coins from your own wallet app, so your keys never leave your device.

Step by step

  1. Choose the sell and buy coins, enter an amount and pick a route: instant exchange or, for BTC ↔ XMR, an atomic swap.
  2. Paste your receiving address for the coin you are buying. For XMR, use the correct subaddress from your own wallet and check it against the coin and network.
  3. Add a refund address for the coin you are selling, so funds can be returned if the swap cannot complete.
  4. Review the quote, then send the exact amount from your own wallet to the deposit address shown (instant exchange) or follow the prompts to lock funds with your counterparty (atomic swap).
  5. Wait for confirmations. The swap proceeds once your transaction has enough confirmations, and the output arrives at your address.

Send only the coin and network the order specifies. A wrong coin or a wrong network sent to a deposit address may not be recoverable.

Fees

What a Monero swap costs

XMR network fee

Every Monero transaction pays a network fee, taken from the amount you send in the same coin. The fee depends on transaction size and priority, and your wallet shows it before you send. Sending BTC or ETH carries that network's own fee as well.

Exchange or provider margin

An instant-exchange route builds its fee or spread into the quoted rate, so the amount you receive is the figure that matters. In an atomic swap, the liquidity provider sets the rate you are shown.

Confirmations and time

Monero transactions need confirmations before the swap proceeds, and received XMR stays locked for a short period before it can be spent. This waiting time is separate from fees, and atomic swaps add further steps on both chains.

Compare the final amount

Quotes can change between routes and over time. Compare the expected received amount, not only the stated rate, and check the minimum and maximum for the pair.

Security

Staying safe

Non-custodial interface

This interface never holds your private keys and does not take custody of your wallet. You receive funds at an address you control. During an instant exchange, however, the service temporarily holds your deposit until it pays out, so that route carries service risk that an atomic swap does not.

Before you send

  • Copy the receiving address from your own wallet and verify the full string, including the first and last characters.
  • Use the correct Monero subaddress for the wallet you want to receive in, and set a refund address you control.
  • Send only the exact coin and network the order shows, and keep the order ID until the swap completes.
  • Atomic swaps depend on staying online and following each step, so do not close the swap midway.
  • Follow the laws and tax rules where you live. This interface gives no advice on evading any rule.
FAQ

Monero Swap FAQ

How does a Monero swap work?

Because Monero is not an EVM chain, a Monero swap goes through an instant-exchange service or a BTC–XMR atomic swap. With an exchange you send coins to a deposit address and receive the other coin at your own address. An atomic swap links the Monero and Bitcoin chains directly with a counterparty.

Can I swap XMR to ETH or USDT with an atomic swap?

No. Atomic swaps in this context cover BTC and XMR only. For XMR against ETH or USDT, use the instant-exchange route, which relies on the service temporarily while the swap runs.

Why does the swap wait before it starts?

Monero transactions need confirmations before the swap proceeds, to reduce the risk of a transaction being reversed. Waiting time depends on the network, so plan for a delay between sending and receiving.

What is the refund address for?

It is an address you control in the coin you are selling. If a swap cannot complete, for example because the amount is out of range, funds are returned there. Always set it yourself and double-check it.

Do I need a subaddress to receive XMR?

Using a subaddress is recommended, since it lets you separate incoming payments while keeping them in one wallet. Generate it in your own Monero wallet and paste it exactly. Check that it belongs to Monero and not another coin.

Is a Monero swap legal?

Rules for buying, selling and swapping crypto differ by country, and you are responsible for following the laws and tax rules where you live. This page is informational and does not give legal or financial advice.
